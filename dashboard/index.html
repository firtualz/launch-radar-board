Research note

Method, in plain words

Start from the 342 protocols in the snapshot that record no token (no market cap, no CoinGecko id, no symbol). Drop the ones that are not a launch candidate: exchanges, bridges, staking products, and names that say "bridge", "wrapped" or "staked", or a versioned slug. That leaves 202 clean protocols. The board carries all 202, ranked by implied value — every eligible pre-token protocol in the snapshot, not a sample of it.

Implied value comes from a joint ridge regression fitted to the 81 protocols that already trade: log10(market cap) on log10(TVL), log10(age), the 7-day change, log10(chain count), and seven category dummies (Dexs is the reference). Features are standardised; λ = 30 by 5-fold cross-validation. The OLS log-log curve (one line on log TVL) is kept as the baseline, fitted on the same 81 comps, so the comparison is apples to apples. A category gets a dummy only when it is one of the seven tested categories; the dummy set was chosen on nested leave-one-out error, not by hand. Farm cost is priced from a fixed table of gas and time per chain; ROI is the implied value per dollar of farm cost, shown as an index where the median protocol scores 100. Every implied value carries a 90% bootstrap band (1000 case resamples of the comps, the ridge refit each time).

What the data says

  1. The ridge beats the single line, and the comparison is on out-of-sample numbers. OLS: R² 0.281, leave-one-out median error 85.94%, median fold 2.94×. Ridge: R² 0.392 in-sample, nested leave-one-out (λ re-chosen inside every fold) median error 77.60%, median fold 2.93×. Walk-forward — fit the 42 comps listed longest ago (617–1850 days), test the 19 most recently listed (80–596 days) — ridge median error 66.35%, OLS 69.71%.
  2. The curve cuts the giants. Sentora Curator is rank 1 at $216.3M; the starter's flat formula (0.25× TVL with growth and maturity) says $522.2M, and the OLS curve alone says $108.2M. Large protocols trade at low multiples: Lido's market cap is 0.013× its TVL. The ridge sits between the two because the category term lifts Risk Curators (see point 5).
  3. Sentora is rank 1, not Steakhouse, and the gap is chains. Sentora holds $2.758B TVL, Steakhouse $2.754B — nearly identical — yet Sentora is $216.3M against Steakhouse's $179.2M. Both are Risk Curators, so the category term is the same (+0.65 dex). The gap is the chain count: Sentora runs on 4 chains, Steakhouse on 11, and the ridge's chain coefficient is negative, so the extra chains cost Steakhouse ~0.09 dex (~20%). TVL alone does not decide the top.
  4. USDD is a stablecoin, not a farm. USDD sits at rank 12 with an implied $39.2M, but it is flagged as a stablecoin: the curve was fitted without stablecoins, so its implied value is not comparable to the others and it is not a farm recommendation. Its verdict is skip.
  5. The Risk Curators premium is the least certain number on the board — and the board says so. It rests on a single trading comp, api3, whose market-cap/TVL ratio is 2.975 against a 0.205 median (a +0.97 dex residual off the TVL curve). The dummy gives every Risk Curators row +0.65 dex (~4.5×), which is why the top three rows are all Risk Curators. The 90% bands carry the warning: Sentora's band is 11.7× wide, Steakhouse's 12.5×, Gauntlet's 13.7×. A premium estimated from one protocol is not a fact; it is the model's best guess, and the band is how wide that guess is.
  6. The filter buys honesty, not accuracy. Dropping 47 comps (38 by category, 9 by ratio) lifts the OLS R² from 0.194 to 0.281 and cuts the mean error from 986% to 327%, but the typical error does not move: median 85.9% either way. The filter removes the absurd ratios (one comp trades at 400× its TVL), not the noise.
  7. The ridge under-predicts the newest listings. On the walk-forward test the median signed error is −0.473 dex: the newest protocols trade at higher multiples than the older comps the curve was fitted on, so the ridge prices young protocols ~3× low. Its implied values for young protocols are conservative, not generous.
  8. Coverage is complete, and the loose count is wrong. Another 272 protocols record no market cap but DO have a token (a CoinGecko id or a symbol — AAVE, AERO and the like). They trade, so they are live, not pre-token, and the board does not rank them. Counting them would put live-token protocols on a pre-token board.

Verdict: farm, thin or skip

Every row carries a verdict, from the snapshot alone — a transparent rule, not a prediction that a protocol will airdrop. The rule uses the ROI index (median 100), the stablecoin flag, TVL, listing age and the 7-day change: farm when the ROI index is at least 200, the 7-day change is at least −20%, TVL is at least $10M and the protocol was listed at least 90 days ago; skip when it is a stablecoin, or the ROI index is below 50, or the 7-day change is below −30%, or TVL is below $10M while the ROI index is below 100; thin otherwise. On this board: 33 farm, 106 thin, 63 skip. Six high-ROI rows are only thin because a farm gate blocks them: nonce-capital (ROI 951, listed 45 days ago), keyrock (416, 73 days), flowdesk (267, falling −27.6% in 7 days), waterline (243, 72 days), gamma-research (411, 72 days), and USDD (354, a stablecoin). The verdict measures value efficiency against cost from the snapshot, not a guarantee of any result.

Limitations

  • The ridge explains only 39% of the variation (R² 0.392). Typical error is ~2.9×, and a quarter of comps are off by 5.7× or more. The 90% band on the median board row is 4.85× wide; at the median comp TVL it is 1.92× wide ($5.8M–$11.1M). Treat every implied value as a yardstick, not a price target.
  • The board is TVL-driven: the curve prices a launch mostly from TVL (the logTVL coefficient is 0.31, the largest by far), so the top rows are the largest protocols. A small protocol with a strong community can out-earn its implied value, and a large one can launch at a fraction of it.
  • The Risk Curators premium rests on one comp (see point 5 above). The top of the board is the least certain part of it.
  • Farm steps are built from each protocol's own snapshot data — its name, TVL, age, 7-day change, verdict, chains, and category — and cite it in the step; the main action, hold, and verdict steps cite the numbers directly. Farm steps are not an audit of each protocol's mechanism. Every mechanism step says "if the protocol offers it" and tells the farmer to check the protocol's own docs first.
  • The data is frozen at 14 November 2026. TVL, listings and tokens change; a protocol that launches after the snapshot keeps its row here.
